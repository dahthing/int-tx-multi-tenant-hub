## 1. What do surveys and reports say are the biggest problems and priorities of ecommerce businesses in Portugal and Spain?

### Takeaway
No 2026 survey ranks the operational pains of SMB merchants in PT or ES. The new 2026 data is thin.
- **Spain:** the main merchant-side source is Packlink's *Ecommerce Delivery Benchmark Report* (March 2026). It names **technology adoption (36%)** and **rising order handling and preparation costs (28%)** as the top strategic challenges, even though 74% of Spanish companies plan to grow online sales.
- **Portugal:** the CTT sources (barometer, annual report) and coverage of the end of the EU de minimis exemption (1 July 2026) dominate. In 2026 the new pressure is regulatory and competitive, not operational.

Across sources, three themes repeat:
- cost to serve: shipping, preparation, returns and damage;
- coping with technology and skills;
- a growing pile of compliance deadlines in 2027: PT qualified e-signature on PDF invoices, ES VeriFactu, and ES's new customer-service law.

### Cited Findings
**Spain: 2026 merchant surveys**
- Packlink *Ecommerce Delivery Benchmark Report* (published March 2026):
  - **74% of Spanish companies plan to increase online sales in 2026**, ahead of Germany (66%) and Italy (54%) but behind the US (90%) and UK (80%).
  - The top strategic challenges are **technology adoption (36%)** and **rising order handling/preparation costs (28%)**.
  - Optimising shipping costs and offering flexible delivery are named as keys to staying competitive.
  - Sample: **8,000+ industry professionals plus 400 executives of companies with more than $12.5M revenue**, so not SMB-only.
  - **[SURVEY-V]** — [PuroMarketing](https://www.puromarketing.com/76/216860/espana-lidera-optimismo-ecommerce-europeo-2026-frente-retos-costes-operativos)
- DHL *E-commerce Trends Report 2026*:
  - Sample: 29,000 online shoppers and **5,800 ecommerce businesses in 28 countries**, fieldwork 15 Dec 2025 to 11 Feb 2026.
  - Core finding: an "expectation gap" between what shoppers demand and what online businesses are ready to deliver, which DHL says challenges retailers' **costs and margins**.
  - Nearly one-third of shoppers would let AI make purchases within five years.
  - I could not retrieve the country split or the full ranking of business challenges, because the report is gated.
  - **[SURVEY-V, multi-country]** — [DHL press release](https://group.dhl.com/en/media-relations/press-releases/2026/dhl-ecommerce-trends-report-2026-old-rules-do-not-apply-in-the-age-of-ai.html); [DHL PR PDF](https://group.dhl.com/content/dam/deutschepostdhl/en/media-relations/press-releases/2026/pr-dhl-trends-report-2026-20260602.pdf)

**Portugal: CTT sources and the 2026 regulatory shock**
- *CTT e-Commerce Report 2025*:
  - Fieldwork May–June 2025, with **500 e-buyers in PT and 1,000 in ES** (consumer side).
  - InvoiceXpress's write-up says **logistics and returns are gaining weight in the purchase decision**.
  - **[SURVEY]** — [CTT Express report page](https://www.cttexpress.com/wp-content/imgmails/e-commerce-report-2025); [InvoiceXpress 2026 trends](https://invoicexpress.com/blog/tendencias-comercio-eletronico-2026/)

  - **[PRESS/HARD]** — [Observador, 28 Jul 2026](https://observador.pt/2026/07/28/ctt-baixam-lucros-no-semestre-que-teve-desafio-acrescido-com-a-taxa-europeia-para-encomendas-abaixo-de-150-euros/); [ECO News](https://econews.pt/?p=44487)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** Put together with the earlier file, the PT/ES survey evidence ranks merchant priorities roughly like this:
  1. Cost to serve: shipping and preparation, then returns and damage. This is the most consistently cited pain in ES.
  2. Technology and skills gaps.
  3. Compliance.
  4. Growth through marketplaces and AI/data, which is the stated investment priority in PT.
- **[INFERENCE]** The de minimis change mostly helps EU-based SMBs against Temu and Shein. For a hub, the takeaway is that 2026–2027 Iberian SMB stress is about **margins and compliance, not demand**. That favours a product pitched as "protect margin, stay compliant, save time" over "grow sales".
- **[INFERENCE]** The weakest link in the evidence is that the **merchant-side samples are tiny or skewed to larger firms**: CTT's panel of about 50, and Packlink's 400 executives at firms with more than $12.5M revenue. Primary interviews are the only way to get an SMB-true ranking.

### Gaps
- No PT/ES survey from 2025–2026 ranks operational pains among SMB merchants specifically. ACEPI, Adigital, ONTSI-empresas, IAB Spain 2026, Observatorio eCommerce and Ecommerce Europe 2026 country data were not found or were inaccessible.
- The CTT barometer 2026 waves could not be read (fetch failed; search returned no body).
- I found no Eurostat PT/ES country values for "obstacles to web sales" (table isoc_ec_wsobs exists, but values were not surfaced).
- The Packlink report's Spain-only SMB breakdown, and the DHL report's business-challenge ranking and PT/ES split, are both gated.

## 2. Pains by area: evidence, the tools merchants use today, and what they complain about

### Takeaway
Across the nine areas, the strongest new 2026 evidence is in four places:
- **Ads:** European Google Shopping/PMax CPCs rose 15% YoY and ROAS fell 43–46%.
- **Returns and damage:** Spain has €13.3bn of online returns, and damage-related returns hit 88% of online merchants.
- **Delivery failures:** OCU logged about 5,000 courier complaints in 2025, and 66% of buyers would not reorder after a late delivery.
- **Compliance deadlines bunching up in 2027:** PT qualified e-signature on PDF invoices from 1 Jan 2027; ES VeriFactu from Jan/Jul 2027; GPSR listing data since Dec 2024; ES packaging EPR; ES customer-service law.

For stock, the "source of truth" problem shows up concretely in ERP↔PrestaShop integration docs and forum threads, not in surveys: SKU matching, maintenance mode, orders decrementing stock twice, bundles, and sync lags of 15 minutes to 2 hours. Profitability, cash flow, decision-making and customer service have almost no PT/ES-specific evidence. The available data is from vendors or from outside Iberia.

### Cited Findings

#### (1) Stock, overselling and the source-of-truth problem
- **How ERP↔webshop sync breaks (Holded–PrestaShop, ES).** Holded's own documentation says:
  - Stock sync is **bidirectional only if SKUs match on both sides and the Inventory add-on is active**.
  - If the shop is in maintenance mode, Holded **cannot push to it**, so only PrestaShop→Holded updates happen.
  - There is **no option to stop PrestaShop orders from decrementing Holded stock**; the documented workaround is a "virtual warehouse".
  - Without stock sync, stock changes only when documents are created: an invoice or ticket decrements stock, a sales order does not until it is shipped.
  - **Packs/bundles do not import correctly**, and invoice numbering may not match.
  - **[VENDOR docs]** — [Holded stock sync FAQ](https://help.holded.com/en/articles/8123740-prestashop-stock-synchronisation-frequently-asked-questions); [Holded sales sync FAQ](https://help.holded.com/en/articles/8123731-prestashop-sales-synchronisation-frequently-asked-questions); [Holded shop connection FAQ](https://help.holded.com/en/articles/6984250-prestashop-synchronisation-and-connection-of-shops-frequently-asked-questions)
- **Portugal, Sage 50 forum.** A user of a Sage↔PrestaShop add-on ("Pasta Digital" 3.6.3.0) reports that orders and price changes sync but **stock does not, even when sync is forced**. The log shows an error sending stock to the PrestaShop 8.1.1 API. **[ANECDOTE, PT]** — [Sage Community Hub PT](https://communityhub.sage.com/pt/sage-50cloud-portugal/f/forum-geral/262368/addon-e-commerce-sage-by-pasta-digital-3-6-3-0---erro-stocks-prestashop-8-1-1)
- An ERP integrator (ES) warns that **a sync error can cause order problems, stock errors "and even economic losses"**, and recommends testing in staging. **[VENDOR]** — [Labelgrup](https://www.labelgrup.com/mejores-practicas-para-la-sincronizacion-de-prestashop-y-erp)
- A PrestaShop stock-sync module (ES vendor) describes the multi-shop failure: several shops depend on one warehouse, a product sells, and **only while handling the order do you find it is already sold out**. **[VENDOR]** — [Alabazweb](https://www.alabazweb.com/en/addons-from-prestashop/417-sync-stock.html)
- Stockagile (an ES inventory tool) lets merchants sync products, stock or prices **separately "so changes don't overwrite each other"**, which shows that overwrite conflicts are a known failure mode. **[VENDOR]** — [Stockagile PrestaShop sync options](https://support.stockagile.com/en/articles/11010779-sync-options-in-prestashop); [Stockagile Amazon](https://intercom.help/stockagile-e7f381ccd046/es/articles/10477168)
- **Sync lag in a popular tool.** Sellbrite syncs inventory **every 2 hours on the free plan and every 15 minutes on paid plans**, with no real-time option, and reviewers link this to overselling. **[VENDOR review, NON-IBERIAN]** — [WiserReview](https://wiserreview.com/blog/sellbrite-alternatives/). A separate vendor says a tool that syncs only every 15–60 minutes can still oversell. **[VENDOR]** — [SKU.io](https://www.sku.io/?p=2066)
- **Unverified frequency and cost claim.** A vendor guide claims that merchants on 3+ channels without automated sync oversell **2–4 times a month, at €50–€200+ per incident**. No method is given. **[VENDOR, unreliable]** — [Koongo](https://www.koongo.com/?p=21265)
- **Small retailers' inventory discipline.** Global Payments' *2025 Retail Inventory Report* (200 small retailers, probably US) found that **nearly 1 in 4 learn an item is out of stock only when a customer tries to buy it**, and **over half rely on manual counts and lack an inventory plan**. **[SURVEY-V, NON-IBERIAN]** — [Global Payments](https://www.globalpayments.com/insights/improve-inventory-visibility)
- **Tools in use today (PT/ES), from this session plus the earlier files:**
  - PrestaShop back office, with stock kept in PT invoicing/ERP tools (Moloni, Sage 50, PHC GO) or ES ones (Holded, Stockagile).
  - Feed and marketplace connectors (Koongo, Channable, Lengow, Base.com, Sellbrite).
  - Spreadsheets.
  - **Complaints:** sync lag, SKU-mapping dependence, add-on fragility after PrestaShop upgrades, bundles and variants, and the double-decrement semantics between ERP and shop.

#### (2) Catalogue and product data across channels (listings, PT/ES translations, images, attributes, compliance fields)
- **GPSR (Regulation (EU) 2023/988), in force since 13 Dec 2024.**
  - It applies to anyone selling non-food consumer products in the EU, **on marketplaces and on their own webshop**.
  - Sellers must name an **EU responsible person**, show the manufacturer's name, the importer if relevant, and contact details, and give safety warnings.
  - **Marketplaces require these fields in listings** and have started demanding compliance documents from sellers.
  - Marketplaces must remove listings or suspend traders when ordered to.
  - **[VENDOR/legal blogs]** — [EaseCert](https://easecert.com/es/blogs/perspectivas/como-afecta-gpsr-a-los-vendedores-en-linea); [Complir](https://www.complir.io/es/resources/guides/gpsr-compliance-guide-product-companies); [Freshfields](https://riskandcompliance.freshfields.com/post/102jrau/new-general-product-safety-regulation-gpsr-set-to-transform-the-eu-product-comp); [InvoiceXpress (PT, for online shops)](https://invoicexpress.com/blog/novo-regulamento-geral-seguranca-produtos-lojas-online/); [ChannelEngine](https://channelengine.com/en/blog/general-product-safety-regulation-gpsr-compliance)
- **Localisation.** A study cited in cross-border coverage concludes that **simply translating a platform is not enough to satisfy local consumers**, and lists cross-border expansion among the biggest difficulties for European online stores. **[PRESS citing a study; Brazilian outlet]** — [Monitor Mercantil](https://monitormercantil.com.br/expansao-para-outros-paises-e-entrave-no-e-commerce-europeu/)
- **Tools in use and their complaints: Channable.**
  - Capterra: **4.6/5 from 96 reviews, ease of use 4.3, value for money 4.4**.
  - Cons from reviewers: pricing "a bit steep for large catalogues" (but worth the time saved), the vendor "focused on upsells", support weak for some.
  - OMR (236 reviews) summarises: **complex pricing, time-consuming initial setup, limited customisability and occasional data-transfer issues**.
  - **[REVIEWS]** — [Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/182568/channable); [OMR Reviews](https://omr.com/en/reviews/product/channable/all)
- **Feed quality now drives ad cost too.** Agency guides say weak feeds and poor creative waste Performance Max budget. **[VENDOR]** — [StoreYa PMax guide 2026](https://www.storeya.com/ppc/performance-max-ecommerce-guide-2026). The earlier file covers EAN-first Mirakl catalogues, category attributes and Lengow's pricing.

#### (3) Orders, shipping costs, carriers, delivery promises and returns
- **Rising fulfilment cost (ES, 2026).** Rising order handling and preparation cost is the #2 strategic challenge (28%) in Packlink 2026 (see Q1). **[SURVEY-V]** — [PuroMarketing](https://www.puromarketing.com/76/216860/espana-lidera-optimismo-ecommerce-europeo-2026-frente-retos-costes-operativos)

  - **[SURVEY/consumer org]** — [OCU complaints ranking](https://www.ocu.org/consumo-familia/compras-online/noticias/quejas-mensajeria); [OCU delivery survey](https://www.ocu.org/consumo-familia/compras-online/noticias/entregas-compras-encuesta)

- **Returns, by value (ES).** The ZigZag/Retail Economics benchmark estimates Spanish **online returns at €13.3bn by end-2025**. Category return rates are highest in **clothing (31%) and footwear (27%)**. **[SURVEY-V]** — [Law&Trends](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/consumidores/las-devoluciones-online-en-espana-superan-los-13-300-millones-en-2025-1.html)

  - **[SURVEY-V; DS Smith sells packaging]** — [PuroMarketing](https://www.puromarketing.com/76/216610/comercio-online-espana-cierra-millones-articulos-devueltos-danos-durante-envio); [Alimarket](https://www.alimarket.es/envase/noticia/419911/el-ecommerce-en-espana-cierra-el-ano-con-15-2-m-de-articulos-devueltos-por-danos-durante-el-envio)
- **Return-cost rules.** Cyberclick's summary says the customer bears return costs if they did not choose the cheapest delivery option, provided they were properly informed. **[VENDOR summary of law]** — [Cyberclick](https://www.cyberclick.es/numerical-blog/devoluciones-en-ecommerce-como-gestionarlas-de-forma-efectiva-y-no-sea-un-drama). **[PRIOR]** That summary seems to merge two separate rules in Spain's consumer law: the seller need not refund the *extra* cost of a delivery method above the cheapest standard one, and the customer pays the direct return cost only if informed beforehand.   - **Canarias, Ceuta and Melilla need export formalities** that many sellers do not expect.
  - **[VENDOR]** — [Landmark Global](https://landmarkglobal.com/eu/es/noticias/la-forma-mas-barata-de-realizar-envios-internacionales-desde-espana/)
- **Tools in use today:**
  - Packlink PRO (pay per label), Sendcloud (returns portal and multi-carrier labels), Outvio and ShippyPro. See the earlier file for prices. Sendcloud said shipping was "the biggest problem" for Spanish online retailers when it entered Spain (undated, old). **[VENDOR]** — [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/eindhoven-based-scaleup-sendcloud-receives-multimillion-euro-investment-launches-in-spain/); [Sendcloud returns portal](https://www.sendcloud.com/es/portfolio/portal-de-devoluciones/)
  - Portugal: I found no PT-specific return-rate or carrier-complaint data in this session.

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE] The source-of-truth problem is structural, not a bug.** Holded's and Sage's integrations treat the ERP as the fiscal document engine and the shop as an order source, with stock as a side effect: orders decrement stock and so do invoices, unless SKUs match exactly. Add a marketplace connector polling every 15 minutes to 2 hours and you get three ledgers with different update triggers. A hub with **one stock ledger plus reservation, pushing absolute quantities to the shop, the marketplaces and the ERP**, removes the ambiguity. It also has to cope with the pitfalls the ERP docs list: bundles, variants, maintenance mode and numbering.
- **[INFERENCE] 2027 is a compliance cliff that drives buying decisions.** For PT and ES merchants the 2027 deadlines fall within the same window: PT qualified e-signature (1 Jan), ES VeriFactu (1 Jan / 1 Jul), PPWR (Aug 2026), GPSR (ongoing). Merchants will re-evaluate their invoicing and catalogue stack in the next 6–12 months. A hub that **routes every channel's orders to a certified or VeriFactu-compliant invoicing tool** has a timely pitch, e.g. Amazon/Worten/KuantoKusta order → Moloni/InvoiceXpress certified invoice in PT; webshop order → VeriFactu-compliant invoice in ES. It should not become the invoicing software itself.
- **[INFERENCE] GPSR and EPR turn PIM into compliance.** Responsible person, manufacturer contact, warnings, and packaging material and weight per SKU are attributes every channel now wants. A PIM-lite that stores them once and maps them to each channel's template is easier to justify than "nicer descriptions".
- **[INFERENCE] Ads: the pain is margin, not the platform.** With CPC +15% and ROAS −43/−46%, a merchant judging ads on platform ROAS cannot tell whether campaigns are profitable after fees, shipping and returns. The hub's value is **profit-on-ad-spend per SKU and channel** built from data it already holds, plus feed quality. That is cheaper to build than a bid manager, and PMax's automation makes bid managers less relevant anyway.
- **[INFERENCE] Returns and damage are under-served for SMBs.** €13.3bn of returns, damage affecting 88% of merchants, and OCU's complaint volume all point to **returns status that feeds stock (restock only after inspection; see the ChannelDock note in the earlier file) and carrier-incident tracking**. Both are natural extensions of the order module.

## 4. Which pains are acute (cost money or risk the business), which are frequent (daily friction), and which do merchants already pay to solve? Ranked inventory and hub fit

### Takeaway
The pains that are acute, frequent and already paid for are:
1. cross-channel stock truth and overselling;
2. order handling plus shipping, carrier choice and delivery failure;
3. invoicing every channel's orders in certified or compliant software.

Catalogue/listing data is frequent and paid for through feed tools, and in 2026 it is acute again because of compliance fields (GPSR, EAN, packaging). Profit visibility across channels and SKUs, ad efficiency, returns and decision support are **acute in money terms but latent**: merchants feel the margin squeeze (CPC +15%, ROAS −46%, returns and damage over €1bn in ES) but rarely pay a dedicated SMB tool for it. That makes them **differentiators and retention features** for a hub that already holds the order, stock and cost data, not entry products. Customer service across channels is daily friction with low regulatory pressure on SMBs. Cash-flow and payout pain has no Iberian evidence.

### Cited Findings
