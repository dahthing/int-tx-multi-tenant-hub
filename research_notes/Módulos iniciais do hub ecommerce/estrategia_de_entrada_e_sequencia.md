## 1. Origin stories and expansion paths: what was the first module, who was the first segment, and why did the wedge work?

### Takeaway
Almost every successful ecommerce-ops platform began with one narrow, frequent, painful workflow, tied to a specific channel or disruption and often built by a seller or for a seller. Orders, inventory and shipping wedges (BaseLinker, Linnworks, Veeqo, ShipStation, ChannelAdvisor's eBay tool) later grew into listings, shipping and warehouse modules. Feed and marketing wedges (Lengow, Channable) grew into marketplaces, orders and price intelligence. The analytics wedge (Triple Whale) worked because a market shock created the pain (iOS 14 broke Facebook attribution), and it later grew into AI agents and automation. BaseLinker is the closest analogue to this project: a tool for sellers on one dominant local marketplace, a permanent free plan capped by orders, per-order pricing and word-of-mouth growth.

### Inferences
- **Two families of wedge with different growth paths.** (a) The *operations* family (BaseLinker, Linnworks, Veeqo, ShipStation, early ChannelAdvisor) started with order handling, inventory sync or shipping, which are high-frequency and transactional. It then added listings and warehousing. (b) The *catalog/marketing* family (Lengow, Channable, Plytix, Sales Layer) started with product data going out to channels. It then added marketplace orders, ads and price intelligence. Triple Whale is a third, *analytics* path. It worked only because a disruption made existing data untrustworthy for high-ad-spend DTC brands.
- **Founder–seller proximity recurs**: BaseLinker (built for a friend on Allegro), Linnworks (founders were online sellers), Shopify (the founder's own shop), Veeqo (sellers' cost pain). The solo founder's PrestaShop + Worten context fits this pattern.
- **BaseLinker is the strongest analogue, and also the most direct competitor.** It anchored on one dominant regional marketplace (Allegro), the way Worten is the leading Mirakl marketplace in PT. It grew by word of mouth, kept a permanent free plan capped by orders, and charged per order. It **already integrates Worten**, so the decided wedge competes head-on with a mature, free-up-to-100-orders tool.
- None of these companies started as an "all-in-one hub". The all-in-one position came from expansion and M&A (Linnworks + SkuVault, ChannelAdvisor + CommerceHub, Lengow + Netrivals). This argues for a sharp first module whose data model is designed to expand.

- No retention or ARR data for any of these companies split by module.

## 2. Dependency chain: which data and capabilities are prerequisites for which modules, and what is the minimal source of truth?

### Takeaway
Every downstream module depends on a canonical SKU identity: one master SKU/EAN mapped to each channel's offer or product ID, plus stock and cost. Orders → metrics works with only a thin catalog. Mirakl order acceptance in practice needs stock visibility. Listings/feeds need rich, per-marketplace attribute data, the most expensive layer. Ads need a product feed plus margin plus conversion data. The minimal source of truth is "thin PIM + stock ledger + orders", not a full PIM.

### Cited Findings
- On Mirakl, accept/refuse runs per order line (`PUT /api/orders/{order_id}/accept`, OR21). The body is an array of lines, each with an `accepted` boolean — [Yoox/Mirakl integration notes](https://help.useresponse.com/hemi/knowledge-base/article/marketplaces-yoox-technical-scope-yoox-order-management-yoox-order-acknowledgement_1). The Mirakl Connect v2 equivalent is `PUT /v2/orders/{order_id}/accept`. It applies to lines awaiting acceptance, is meant to be called once per new order, and replaces a deprecated v1 — [Mirakl developer docs v2](https://developer.mirakl.com/content/product/connect/rest/connect/openapi3/orders/v2-acceptorderlines); [deprecated v1](https://developer.mirakl.com/content/product/connect/rest/connect/openapi3/orders/acceptorderlines)
- Accept and refuse are only possible while the order is in `WAITING_ACCEPTANCE`. `REFUSED` and `CANCELED` are separate statuses — [Lengow help: Worten (Mirakl) orders](https://help.lengow.com/hc/en-us/articles/12199842176924-Worten-Mirakl-Orders-Management)
- Acceptance deadlines vary by operator. La Redoute (Mirakl) cancels orders not accepted within 24 h, and Lengow recommends turning on automatic acceptance — [Lengow help: La Redoute](https://help.lengow.com/hc/en-us/articles/12876572315292-La-Redoute-Mirakl-Orders-Management). Stripe's Mirakl connector says orders must be accepted or refused within 7 days, the validity of the card authorization — [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/connectors/mirakl/payments)
- Mirakl listing connectors must map products to each operator's category tree, attribute schema and mandatory value lists, with per-operator overrides "where two marketplaces demand different data". Mappings are validated in the operator's sandbox before go-live — [ECOSIRE Odoo Mirakl connector](https://ecosire.com/apps/odoo/mirakl-marketplace-connector); [ECOSIRE Magento Mirakl connector](https://ecosire.com/apps/magento-2/mirakl-connect-integration-magento2)
- Lengow's Worten onboarding bundles catalog preparation, category mapping and price/title optimization *before* stock sync and order handling, with a 2–6 week launch — [Lengow Worten](https://www.lengow.com/fr/integrations/worten)
- Linnworks' first version was inventory sync across channels; order management, listings, shipping and warehouse (SkuVault) came later — [Linnworks About](https://linnworks.com/about-us/)
- Rithum (ChannelAdvisor) combined marketplace and digital-advertising management. The ads business ($500M+ annual ad spend managed) sat on top of the product-feed and marketplace catalog layer — [Wikipedia: Rithum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rithum)
- Triple Whale had to build its own first-party pixel because platform-reported ad data was unreliable. Ads decision support needs independent conversion data, not just the ad platforms' numbers — [TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/02/triple-whale-raises-25m-for-its-shopify-data-platform/)

### Inferences
- **Dependency graph (own synthesis):**
  - `Channel connections + SKU identity map` → **Orders import** (needs only the channel SKU ↔ master SKU link)
  - `Orders + per-SKU cost + marketplace commission/fees + shipping cost` → **Order and margin metrics** (without cost data, metrics are revenue-only, which marketplaces already show)
  - `Orders + stock per SKU` → **Accept/refuse decisions** (a refusal is essentially "no stock" or "unprofitable"). The decided wedge therefore already needs at least *read* stock from PrestaShop.
  - `Stock ledger + SKU map + push to each channel` → **Stock sync / oversell prevention** → `locations, reservations, picking` → **WMS**
  - `Rich attributes + media + per-operator category/value-list mapping` → **Listings/feeds (PIM)**
  - `Product feed + margin per SKU + conversion/attribution data` → **Ads optimization / AI ads advice**
- **Minimal source of truth for v1** (inference): a `Product/SKU` master (master SKU, EAN, title, cost price, VAT), a `ChannelListing` link (channel, channel SKU/offer ID, price, status), a `StockLevel` per location with movement history, and `Order`/`OrderLine` records with fees. That is enough for orders, metrics, accept/refuse and later stock sync. Rich content attributes can be added when the PIM/listings module arrives.
- **A cheaper path to listings** (background knowledge about Mirakl, not verified this session): Mirakl lets sellers create *offers* (price/stock) on products already in the operator's catalog, matched by EAN. This avoids full product creation and attribute mapping. An "offers-only" listing feature would cost far less than full PIM-to-marketplace product creation and could be the bridge between the orders wedge and full feeds.
- **Human approval vs deadlines**: with deadlines as short as 24 h on some Mirakl operators and Lengow recommending auto-accept, an "always human approval" flow carries cancellation risk. Better: auto-accept by rule (stock available, margin above threshold), with humans and the AI handling only exceptions.

### Gaps
- Worten's own acceptance deadline and refusal/cancellation thresholds were not found. Search returned consumer complaints and other platforms' rules. Check the Worten seller terms.
- No official Mirakl doc retrieved on the offer-only vs product-creation flows, or on per-operator catalog differences.

## 3. Which wedge produces daily usage and retention, and which only occasional use?

### Takeaway
There is no published head-to-head churn data by module type for SMB ecommerce tools. Indirect evidence (marketplace acceptance deadlines, the daily order flow, vendors' framing of inventory sync as their core) suggests orders and stock are daily, operational workflows that are hard to switch off. Feed/PIM tools are set-up-heavy and then periodic: sticky through switching cost (mappings) more than through daily use. Dashboards/analytics are the least frequent and have the weakest willingness to pay, unless tied to a costly pain such as ad spend (Triple Whale).

### Cited Findings
- Mirakl operators require accept/refuse decisions within 24 h (La Redoute) to 7 days (payment-authorization window). This forces at least daily attention on new orders — [Lengow help: La Redoute](https://help.lengow.com/hc/en-us/articles/12876572315292-La-Redoute-Mirakl-Orders-Management); [Stripe Mirakl connector](https://docs.stripe.com/connectors/mirakl/payments)
- ChartMogul data: top-quartile customer retention is low for low-ARPA SaaS. Two ChartMogul posts give conflicting figures for ARPA under $25/month: 64.7% and 73% "best-in-class". Top-quartile retention is 85.8% for ARPA above $1k/month. Benchmarks come from 2,500+ SaaS businesses on a year-over-year basis — [ChartMogul: good customer churn rate](https://chartmogul.com/blog/good-customer-churn-rate/); [ChartMogul blog](https://chartmogul.com/blog/?p=18718); [ChartMogul benchmarks help](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/138-benchmarks)
- Shopify app churn benchmarks circulate (day-one uninstall of 20–25% "good", 40–45% "hard to satisfy"), but they are unsourced — [Spur-IT guide](https://spur-i-t.com/blog/shopify-app-store-guide-to-revenue-app-metrics/)
- Triple Whale's analytics wedge gained traction with DTC brands whose main pain was ad spend measurement after iOS 14 — [TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/02/triple-whale-raises-25m-for-its-shopify-data-platform/). It is now moving from "dashboards to doing" with AI agents and automations, gated in its paid "Automate" tier — [Triple Whale KB](https://kb.triplewhale.com/en/articles/16046642-understanding-triple-whale-plans-foundation-automate-and-enterprise); [eCommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/?p=206602)
- Consumer complaints on Portuguese complaint sites describe Worten marketplace sellers cancelling accepted orders for "stock shortage" while the item stayed listed. Out-of-sync stock is a visible, recurring problem on Worten — [DECO Proteste complaint](https://www.deco.proteste.pt/reclamar/todas-as-reclamacoes/worten-n-C3-A3o-responsabiliza-se-p/CPTPT01731782-41); [Portal da Queixa](https://portaldaqueixa.com/brands/worten/complaints/worten-cancelamento-indevido-de-encomenda-de-portatil-apos-sucessivos-adiamentos-162344726)

### Inferences
- **Frequency ranking (inference):** orders (daily, deadline-driven) > stock sync (continuous background; noticed when it fails) > metrics (weekly) > listings/feeds (bursty: onboarding and new products) > ads analytics (weekly/monthly, and only for merchants with meaningful ad spend).
- **Two kinds of stickiness:** orders and stock are sticky through *operational dependence*: switching mid-season is risky. Feeds and PIM are sticky through *configuration lock-in* (hundreds of category/attribute mappings). Analytics has neither unless it holds history the merchant cannot easily rebuild, or embeds into decisions (alerts, approvals).
- **Implication for the decided wedge:** the AI orders analyst is more retentive if it is embedded in the daily approval/exception queue ("these 3 lines need a decision, here is why") than if it is a chat over dashboards. That puts AI inside the daily workflow rather than in a weekly-visit surface.
- PT/ES SMB merchants are likely low-ARPA (inference from the founder's "affordable monthly" positioning). ChartMogul's low-ARPA retention data implies roughly 30%+ annual logo churn even for good performers. Retention has to come from daily operational embedding, not from the value of dashboards alone.

### Gaps
- No segmented churn or retention data found for order-management vs feed-management vs analytics tools (searched for the Shopify app ecosystem and SaaS benchmarks).
- No PT/ES-specific data on how often merchants log in to Worten's seller back office, or how many orders per day typical Worten sellers handle.

## 4. Freemium economics in B2B ecommerce SaaS: conversion rates, upgrade triggers and the effect of AI costs

### Takeaway
The benchmark median free-to-paid conversion is about 8% (ChartMogul/ProductLed 2026), with a very wide spread (a quarter of products convert below 2.5%). Ecommerce-ops free tiers almost always cap on **orders/month** (Base 100, Zoho 50, Sellbrite 30). PIM tools cap on SKUs (Plytix 500), analytics and AI tools cap on GMV or AI credits (Triple Whale, Plytix AI credits), and Odoo caps on number of apps. Veeqo is free because Amazon subsidizes it through shipping-label commissions, a model a solo entrant cannot copy. AI inference makes an uncapped free tier risky: AI products run materially lower gross margins, so AI in the free tier has to be metered by credits.

### Cited Findings
- ChartMogul/ProductLed conversion report (2026, ~200 B2B products): median freemium conversion is about 8%. ~25% of products convert below 2.5%, ~29% between 2.5% and 7.5%, and ~25% between 10% and 15%. Measured within 6 months — [ChartMogul SaaS Conversion Report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)
- Median B2B SaaS *trial*-to-paid conversion is 18.5% (a different metric from freemium conversion) — [Flint](https://www.flint.com/blog/b2b-saas-free-trial-conversion-rate-statistics)
- **Base.com:** free up to 100 orders/month, 1,000 products and 3 users. Paid plans combine a fixed fee with per-order charges (figures conflict; see §1) — [European Purpose](https://europeanpurpose.com/tool/base-com); [TrustRadius](https://trustradius.com/products/baselinker/pricing)
- **Sellbrite:** free up to 30 orders/month with unlimited channels and SKUs; Pro from $29/month — [ERP Research](https://erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/marketplace/sellbrite)
- **Zoho Inventory:** the official pricing page shows the free plan at 50 orders, 1 user and 2 locations — [Zoho pricing](https://zstatic.zohostatic.com/inventory/pricing). Older third-party data said 25 online + 25 offline orders and 50 labels/month — [Merchant Maverick](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/reviews/zoho-inventory-review/)
- **Veeqo:** before the Amazon relaunch it averaged ~$450/month. It is now "free for all sellers", who pay only for carrier labels — [Amazon press release, 2022](https://press.aboutamazon.com/news-releases/news-release-details/amazon-launches-veeqo-free-shipping-software-sellers-including). Hybrid pricing (base subscription plus usage-based AI actions) is recommended to protect margin — [ViitorCloud](https://viitorcloud.com/blog/ai-powered-saas-pricing-playbook/). ### Inferences
- **Upgrade triggers that match value:** for an orders-first product, *orders/month* is the natural meter. Base (100) and Zoho (50) set the market expectation, and per-order pricing (Base) scales with merchant revenue, which suits "priced to what merchants can afford". Secondary meters: number of channels (free = 1 shop + 1 marketplace), users, and AI credits.
- **A free tier of ~100 orders/month** matches Base and probably covers most micro-merchants entirely. The free tier then mainly serves acquisition and the agency channel. Conversion at the 8% median would need large free volumes to fund a solo founder, so the paid tier should be set where merchants feel order-handling pain (e.g., more than 100–300 orders/month or more than one marketplace). This threshold is an inference, not evidence.
- **AI in a free tier:** meter it like Plytix (AI credits) or Triple Whale (included Moby usage varying by plan). Free tier: precomputed daily/weekly summaries and anomaly alerts generated in batch with small models, plus a small monthly quota of interactive questions. Interactive analysis and agentic actions belong in paid tiers. Without caps, a few heavy free users can erase the margin, given the 6–9 point compression and ~52% AI gross margins reported for AI-heavy products.
- **Veeqo's "free everything" is not replicable.** It is subsidized by Amazon and label commissions. In PT, the closest analogue would be shipping-label resale (CTT, DPD, etc.), which is a possible later monetization layer, not a v1 plan.
- **Price anchor:** Mirakl Connect starts at $699/month, so a mainstream PT/ES micro or small merchant on Worten is unlikely to adopt it. That leaves room below it. However, Base (free to about €100+/month), the Cognisance Odoo Worten connector (€49.99/month) and Sellbrite ($29+) set the low end.

- No ecommerce-ops-specific free-to-paid conversion data found (e.g., Base, Sellbrite or Zoho conversion rates are not public).
- Secondary figures seen but not tied to a retrievable URL: First Page Sage "3.7% average freemium conversion"; Lenny Rachitsky "6–8% self-serve, 10–15% sales-assisted"; "3–7% monthly SMB churn". Treat them as unverified.

- Cursor's mid-2025 move to credit-based pricing and the backlash that followed is background knowledge, not verified in this session.

## 5. Agency/partner channel: how agencies resell or recommend tools, and what makes them adopt one

### Takeaway
The main European feed and ops tools pay agencies **recurring referral commissions** rather than offering true resale. Channable pays 15–20% of managed clients' revenue, tiered by managed-client volume. Lengow offers certifications, training, premium support and special partner rates. Base.com pays commissions and discounts through referral links. Agencies adopt tools that (a) let them manage many clients from one place, (b) pay recurring revenue, and (c) create billable service work such as feed optimization and marketplace onboarding. This favors the listings/feeds wedge for agency pull and the orders wedge for merchant retention.

### Cited Findings
- **Channable:** recurring revenue "for the clients you actively manage", with commission rising with partner level up to 20%. Levels are Bronze, Silver and Gold, shown as 15% / 17.5% / 20%, and the entry level shows no commission. Levels depend on the number and revenue of managed clients; Silver and Gold are announced once a year (Q1). Commission requires a partner code entered by the account owner. Partners get an agency dashboard for multiple clients, the Channable Academy and product roundtables — [Channable Agency Partner Program](https://www.channable.com/partner-program); [Channable partner code help](https://helpcenter.channable.com/get-started/getting-started-agencies/how-to-use-a-partner-code); [Channable "get started as an agency"](https://helpcenter.channable.com/get-started/getting-started-agencies/get-started-as-an-agency). Sources disagree on how the tiers map to thresholds: the program page lists monthly minimums of 500 / 2,500 / 4,000 — [Channable partner program doc](https://documents.channable.com/partner-program)
- Independent Channable resellers and implementation partners also exist, separate from Channable's own program — [Elioplus](https://elioplus.com/europe/de/germany/channel-partners/channable)
- **Lengow (Spain site):** an agency programme with training, certifications, premium support and "special rates" for partners, pitched as improving agency margins — [Lengow para agencias](https://www.lengow.com/es/sectores-con-los-que-trabajamos/agencia/). Lengow claims Channable has "no professional services (only with agencies)" (vendor claim) — [Lengow comparison](https://lengow.com/?p=61949)
- **Base.com:** a separate partner track for "solution providers and agencies" (consultants, ecommerce agencies, implementers) with commissions, discounts and benefits tied to activity — [Base: Partner with Base](https://base.com/en-US/partners/partner-with-base/); [Base cooperation](https://base.com/en-US/cooperation/). A referral link auto-assigns the sign-up to the partner — [Base help (PL)](https://base.com/pl-PL/pomoc/wiedza/jak-polecac-base-i-przypisywac-uzytkownikow-do-swojego-konta-partnerskiego/). The UK ambassador programme pays commission on paid plans plus bonuses — [Base UK ambassador](https://mkt.base.com/en-gb/uk-ambassador-partner-programme)
- **Shopify (developer side):** app developers keep 100% of their first $1M lifetime revenue, and Shopify takes 15% above that. From 2025 the exemption is lifetime rather than reset annually — [Shopify revenue share docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share); [Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/update-to-shopifys-app-developer-revenue-share)
- A Spanish PrestaShop merchant (efectoLED) case study shows the agency/tool pattern in ES: a quick Lengow module install, then expansion to Amazon, eBay, ManoMano, Fnac and other marketplaces — [Lengow efectoLED case](https://blog.lengow.com/marketplaces/success-story-international-expansion-and-improved-performance-of-efectoled-online/)

### Inferences
- **The market rate for agency incentives is 15–20% recurring**, plus certification/academy and an agency multi-client dashboard. A newcomer may need the top of that range (or a white-label option) to displace incumbents agencies already use.
- **What agencies sell**: marketplace onboarding projects (Lengow's 2–6 week launch), feed optimization and ads management are billable services. An orders inbox is the merchant's own staff's daily tool and creates little agency work. So:
  - The **orders wedge** wins merchants directly and retains them, but gives agencies a weak reason to push it, other than as a free add-on for their clients.
  - The **listings/feeds wedge** gives agencies billable work and strong pull, but costs the most to build (see §6).
  - A middle path (inference): ship orders first for direct merchants, while building the multi-tenant **agency workspace** (one login, many client tenants, white-label reports, commission tracking) early. The multi-tenant architecture already supports this. AI-generated client reports ("monthly marketplace performance for client X") give agencies something billable without full PIM.
- The "agencies can resell" goal implies a reseller/white-label model, which neither Channable nor Base clearly offers (their programs are referral or commission). That could be a differentiator, at the cost of billing complexity (agency-billed tenants).

## 6. Build and maintenance cost of each wedge for a solo developer, and evidence on integration burden

### Takeaway
Order import is the cheapest wedge to build. Mirakl gives one API family across many operators, with simple endpoints (list orders, accept or refuse lines). Stock sync is moderately complex to build but has a high reliability burden: oversells become marketplace cancellations, and the tool gets the blame. Listings/PIM is the most expensive, because per-operator category trees, attributes and value lists must be mapped and maintained. Platform risk is real: Amazon forced a full MWS→SP-API rewrite and then proposed, and withdrew, developer fees. PrestaShop 9's new Admin API is still incomplete, while most shops still run the legacy XML webservice.

### Cited Findings
- **Mirakl orders:** accept/refuse is a single PUT per order with a line array (OR21) — [Yoox/Mirakl notes](https://help.useresponse.com/hemi/knowledge-base/article/marketplaces-yoox-technical-scope-yoox-order-management-yoox-order-acknowledgement_1). Mirakl Connect has a v2 endpoint and a deprecated v1, so expect version migrations — [Mirakl docs v2](https://developer.mirakl.com/content/product/connect/rest/connect/openapi3/orders/v2-acceptorderlines). Mirakl recommends that connectors log action results and errors through an order-actions reporting endpoint — [Mirakl Connect APIs overview](https://developer.mirakl.com/content/product/connect/rest/connect/openapi3.md)
- **Mirakl listings:** operator-specific attribute schemas, mandatory value lists, per-operator overrides, and mappings built per client catalog and validated in each operator's sandbox — [ECOSIRE Odoo connector](https://ecosire.com/apps/odoo/mirakl-marketplace-connector); [ECOSIRE Magento connector](https://ecosire.com/apps/magento-2/mirakl-connect-integration-magento2)
- **Existing Worten connectors (the competition and the price floor):** the Cognisance Odoo connector for Worten PT (`mirakl_worten_pt`) costs €49.99/month with a 14-day trial — [Odoo Apps](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/cognisance_worten_pt). A WooCommerce "Worten Conector" plugin (presented as an official Worten partner) syncs offers (price, stock, VAT, handling time) and, with an optional module, orders and tracking — [WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/worten-conector/). A WooCommerce Mirakl plugin warns that missing acceptance deadlines can auto-cancel orders and offers per-shop auto-accept — [WordPress.org plugin](https://wordpress.org/plugins/?p=358237)
- **Amazon SP-API burden:** Amazon migrated all developers off MWS. Public developers had to finish by December 31, 2022 — [Amazon SP-API news, Feb 2022](https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api-news/docs/february-2022?lang=fr-FR). Private developers had until about March 31 / April 1, 2024 — [Amazon seller forum](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/6403cff0d2c92b4ef066d93210fe51a3). Sellers reported being locked out of SP-API access — [Amazon.in seller forum](https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/dc18fc3d-37ba-4d5a-9674-f3a21d071800). Individual reports were also retired on a schedule — [SP-API changelog](https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/changelog/deprecation-of-get_merchant_listings_defect_data-and-listings_defect_report_xl)
- **Amazon SP-API fees:** in November 2025 Amazon announced a $1,400 annual fee for third-party developers from January 31, 2026, plus GET-call usage tiers from April 30, 2026 (Basic 2.5M calls/month included, Pro $1,000/month, Plus $10,000/month, $0.40 per 1,000 overage calls) — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/amazon-introduces-fees-for-third-party-developer-api-access-in-2026/); [Amazon SPP announcement](https://developer.amazonservices.com/spp-announcement). On about May 12, 2026 Amazon said it "will not move forward with the SP-API usage and annual fees at this time" — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/amazon-drops-sp-api-fees-after-developer-pushback); [Novadata](https://novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-cancels-sp-api-fees-may-2026)
- **PrestaShop 9:** the new Admin API is built on API Platform 3 with OAuth and CQRS endpoints, is enabled by default, and requires HTTPS in production — [PrestaShop devdocs: Admin API](https://devdocs.prestashop-project.org/9/admin-api/); [How to use](https://devdocs.prestashop-project.org/9/admin-api/how-to-use/). It is "still being enriched" — [Ecommerce Nation](https://www.ecommerce-nation.fr/prestashop-9-est-disponible-ce-qui-change/). The legacy webservice (XML by default, JSON optional, HTTP Basic Auth) is reportedly what "95% of running shops" use (vendor claim) — [MyPresta](https://mypresta.rocks/knowledge-base/guides/prestashop-webservice-api). Rate limits are not documented — [Rollout](https://rollout.com/integration-guides/prestashop/api-essentials). A French agency benchmarked Admin API performance, but no figures were retrieved — [Egio Digital](https://www.egiodigital.com/publications/tag/prestashop/)
- **Stock sync reliability:** vendors recommend syncing every 5–15 minutes and say typical default lag is 5–15 minutes, which is too slow for fast sellers — [Koongo](https://www.koongo.com/?p=21265); [EasySellApp](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/shopify-multichannel-inventory-sync-overselling). One vendor claims merchants on 3+ channels without sync oversell 2–4 times a month, at €50–200 per incident (unsourced) — [Koongo](https://www.koongo.com/?p=21265). Amazon asks seller-fulfilled accounts to keep pre-fulfilment cancellations below 2.5% — [ChannelDock](https://channeldock.com/en/blogs/negative-inventory-multichannel-ecommerce/). An older guide puts Rue du Commerce's threshold below 5%/month — [Sellermania](https://www.sellermania.com/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/69892)

### Inferences
- **Relative build/maintenance cost (own estimate, ordinal):**
  1. *Order import + accept/refuse (Mirakl + PrestaShop)*: **low–medium**. Mostly read-heavy polling plus a few writes. Failures are recoverable (re-poll). Mirakl's shared API family means Worten work largely carries over to other Mirakl operators in Iberia, apart from per-operator config. (That other PT/ES marketplaces run on Mirakl is background knowledge, not verified here.)
  2. *Order metrics + AI analyst*: **low** marginal cost on top of orders, but AI inference is an ongoing cost (§4), and margin metrics need cost data the merchant must supply.
  3. *Stock sync*: **medium build, high operational liability**. It needs idempotent, near-real-time pushes (minutes), conflict handling, reservations for pending Mirakl lines, monitoring and alerting. A bug causes real cancellations that hurt the merchant's marketplace standing. For a solo founder, 24/7 reliability is the binding constraint.
  4. *Listings/PIM/feeds*: **high build, high maintenance**. Per-operator category and attribute mapping, value lists, validation errors and sandbox certification all multiply with each marketplace. Incumbents (Lengow, Channable, Base) have years of mappings. An offers-only (EAN-matched) listing feature is a much cheaper subset.
  5. *Ads*: **high**. It needs the feed, margin data, Google/Meta API integrations (each with its own review processes) and attribution logic, which Triple Whale needed custom pixel infrastructure for.
- **PrestaShop integration choice:** the legacy webservice covers most installed shops (including PS 1.7/8 upgraders). The PS9 Admin API is newer and more limited. A PrestaShop *module* (push via hooks) may give lower latency for stock and order events than polling. That is an architecture decision for the founder's team, flagged here only as a cost driver.
- **Platform risk:** Amazon's forced rewrite and the fee proposal-then-withdrawal show that marketplace APIs carry policy risk outside the developer's control. Starting with Mirakl and PrestaShop (open-source, merchant-hosted) and deferring Amazon reduces early exposure.

### Gaps
- No quantified data on Mirakl API maintenance burden (frequency of breaking changes, per-operator divergence) or connector certification requirements. Mirakl's developer portal did not surface certification criteria.
- No PrestaShop 9 Admin API endpoint coverage list for orders and stock was retrieved.
- No hard data on engineering hours for building a Mirakl listings connector.

## 7. Risks of the "orders + metrics + AI agent" wedge vs the alternative wedges

### Takeaway
The decided wedge is the cheapest to build and the most frequently used, but its standalone willingness to pay is weak. Base.com already offers Worten orders plus listings free up to 100 orders/month, cheap Worten connectors exist (€49.99/month on Odoo), and marketplaces show basic sales data in their back offices. Its defensibility therefore rests on (a) PT/ES and PrestaShop depth, (b) AI embedded in the daily decision queue, and (c) a fast path to stock and offers. The PIM/feeds wedge has stronger agency pull but the highest build cost and entrenched competitors. Stock sync has the sharpest pain but the highest reliability liability. Ads has the most dependencies and is a poor first module.

### Cited Findings
- Base.com already provides two-way Worten integration (orders plus bulk offer listing) — [Base help](https://base.com/pl-PL/pomoc/wiedza/worten-marketplace-konfiguracja-integracji/). It has a permanent free plan up to 100 orders/month — [European Purpose](https://europeanpurpose.com/tool/base-com)
- Lengow and Channable both support Worten in PT/ES via Mirakl — [Lengow Worten](https://www.lengow.com/fr/integrations/worten); [Channable help](https://helpcenter.channable.com/hc/en-us/articles/13879501976338-Setting-up-a-Worten-API). Low-cost Worten connectors exist for Odoo (€49.99/month) and WooCommerce — [Odoo Apps](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/cognisance_worten_pt); [WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/worten-conector/)
- Mirakl Connect lets sellers manage messages, orders and returns from one platform and is free to join, but paid plans start at $699/month — [Mirakl for sellers](https://www.mirakl.com/products/mirakl-for-sellers); [Mirakl Connect pricing](https://www.mirakl.com/products/connect/pricing/). A rival vendor claims sellers once accepted Mirakl Connect's limitations because it was "free or relatively inexpensive" (vendor marketing) — [ChannelEngine](https://www.channelengine.com/lp/mirakl-connect-vs-channelengine)
- Low-ARPA SaaS shows weak top-quartile retention (64.7%–73% for ARPA under $25/month in ChartMogul data) — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/blog/good-customer-churn-rate/)
- Plytix's free PIM (500 SKUs) shows that basic PIM is already free in the market, with a steep jump to $499/month — [KI-Syndikat](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/plytix/)
- The analytics-first success case (Triple Whale) depended on a disruption (iOS 14) and a high-ad-spend DTC segment, and has since moved toward agents that "do" rather than dashboards — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/02/triple-whale-raises-25m-for-its-shopify-data-platform/); [eCommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/?p=206602)
- Worten shoppers publicly complain about seller cancellations due to stock shortages — [DECO Proteste](https://www.deco.proteste.pt/reclamar/todas-as-reclamacoes/worten-n-C3-A3o-responsabiliza-se-p/CPTPT01731782-41)

### Inferences
- **Risk matrix (own synthesis):**

| Wedge | Build cost (solo) | Usage frequency | Willingness to pay | Agency pull | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orders + metrics + AI analyst (decided) | Low–med | Daily | Low–med (dashboards commoditized; Base free tier) | Low–med | Undifferentiated vs Base; "nice dashboard" churn; human-approval latency vs 24 h deadlines |
| Stock sync / oversell prevention | Med build, high ops liability | Continuous | Med–high (avoids cancellations and penalties) | Low–med | 24/7 reliability; the tool gets blamed for oversells |
| PIM / feeds / listings | High | Bursty | Med (agencies bill for it) | **High** | Mapping maintenance; incumbents (Lengow, Channable, Base, Plytix free) |
| Ads | High | Weekly | Med–high only for ad-heavy merchants | Med–high | Needs feed + margin + attribution first; Google/Meta API dependence |

- **Mitigations for the decided wedge (inference):**
  1. **Make stock part of the wedge from day 1.** Read PrestaShop stock to power accept/refuse ("refuse only when stock is truly missing"). This turns the accept/refuse flow into oversell prevention at the point of acceptance, the pain Worten shoppers complain about, without yet taking on full bidirectional sync liability. Then add a stock push to Mirakl (offers) as module 2.
  2. **Rule-based auto-accept with exception approval** instead of approving everything by hand, so short deadlines are never missed. The AI explains exceptions and suggests actions (refuse, partial, contact customer).
  3. **Margin, not revenue.** Collect cost price and marketplace commission per SKU so metrics show contribution margin per channel, which Worten's back office and Base's basic stats likely don't (inference; not verified). This is also the prerequisite data for a later Ads module.
  4. **Thin catalog as the source of truth** (SKU/EAN/cost/channel IDs), so offers-only listing is a cheap next step and full PIM can come later.
  5. **Agency workspace early** (multi-tenant) to tap the agency channel despite the orders wedge's weaker agency pull. Benchmark incentives at 15–20% recurring.
- **Suggested sequencing (inference, for the report writer to weigh):** (1) Orders inbox + accept/refuse with stock check + margin metrics + an AI exceptions analyst → (2) stock push to Mirakl and PrestaShop (oversell prevention) → (3) offers-only listing on Mirakl via EAN, with price rules → (4) full PIM/feeds (Google Shopping feed first, as the bridge to Ads) → (5) Ads decision support on top of feed + margin data → (6) WMS (picking, locations) once order volume justifies it. This mirrors the Linnworks/BaseLinker operations path, adds the Lengow/Channable feed layer later, and puts Triple Whale-style analytics on top only once margin and feed data exist.
- **The case for starting with PIM/feeds instead** would rest on agency distribution and on merchants who are *not yet* on Worten (they need listing before they have orders). If the founder's early pipeline is mostly agencies onboarding merchants to marketplaces, that argues for an offers-only listing feature earlier (step 3 moves to 2). This depends on the customer-discovery data held by the founder, not on external evidence.

### Gaps
- No evidence found on PT/ES merchants' willingness to pay for marketplace analytics specifically, or on what Worten's free seller back office shows (reports, export depth).
- Base.com's Portuguese/Spanish market share and support quality in PT were not found. That would sharpen the competitive assessment.
- No data on the share of Worten sellers already using an integrator (Base, Lengow, Channable) versus managing Mirakl's back office by hand. This would size the "no-tool" segment, the most natural free-tier target.
- The search budget ran out before retrieving Lengow/Channable list prices, Base.com's current scale, or the Linnworks–SkuVault rationale.
