# Ecommerce analytics, ads management and AI agents / decision support for SMB merchants: what exists, what it costs, where AI demonstrably adds value (research date: 2026-10-23)

> **Method note for the report writer.** The sandbox blocked direct page fetches (WebFetch/curl got DNS or proxy refusals for shopify.com, mirakl.com, venon.io, fullmetrix.com and others), and the shared web-search budget ran out after about 45 queries. **Every finding below comes from search-result summaries of the cited URLs, not from reading the full page.** Most vendor prices therefore come from third-party aggregators that often contradict each other; those conflicts are flagged inline. Verify any price against the vendor's live page before publishing it. Claude model prices come from Anthropic's bundled API reference (cached 2026-10-06), not from a web search.

## (a) Analytics / attribution / profit tools: pricing, tiers, what they measure, platform support (PrestaShop? marketplaces?)

### Takeaway
Shopify-centric profit/attribution tools form a crowded, tiered market. At the low end, profit trackers bill by order volume ($0 to $35–$149/mo for small stores). At the high end, attribution/BI suites bill by GMV ($219 to $720+/mo, usually with 12-month commitments). Almost none support PrestaShop or Mirakl marketplaces such as Worten. PrestaShop coverage is limited to small modules (Fullmetrix from €9/mo, StoreMind AI €111.99 one-off, ProfitMetrics for Google Ads). I found **no seller-side profit analytics product for Mirakl/Worten**, so that is the clearest white space.

**Shopify native**

### Inferences
- Pricing clusters into two models. **Order-volume** pricing (Lifetimely, BeProfit, TrueProfit, Metorik) fits SMBs: roughly $25–$149/mo for up to about 3,000 orders. **GMV-based with annual lock-in** (Triple Whale, Polar) puts SMBs off. A PT/ES SMB doing a few hundred orders a month already has free or €9–€79 options on Shopify or PrestaShop for single-store profit.
- What is **not** served is **one profit view across PrestaShop plus Mirakl marketplaces (Worten, Fnac PT/ES, Carrefour ES, etc.)**. That means marketplace commission, shipping and returns per order line, reconciled with the own store. BeProfit covers Shopify, Amazon and WooCommerce, but no product found covers PrestaShop plus Mirakl. This is the most defensible wedge for "metrics".
- Fullmetrix not stating PrestaShop 9 support, and StoreMind being a one-off module, suggest the PrestaShop 9 analytics tier is thin and shallow (modules, not SaaS with multi-channel data).

### Gaps
- GA4 (free / GA4 360), Looker Studio (and Pro) and Looker Studio ecommerce templates: not researched before the search budget ran out. No citable figures.

- Peel and Daasity pricing: not found.
- What reporting Worten/Mirakl actually exposes to sellers (back-office reports, API exports of commissions/payouts): not found. Ask Worten seller support or check the Mirakl seller API docs.
- G2/Capterra review themes for these tools: not gathered beyond Triple Whale's Trustpilot.

## (b) Ads management tools for SMBs: pricing, what SMBs use, and whether "Ads management" is a paid module or the platforms' own automation dominates

### Takeaway
Platform-native AI automation (Google Performance Max / AI Max plus the new Gemini "Advisor" agents, and Meta Advantage+) now carries most SMB ad spend and is free. Third-party optimizers price by ad spend ($0–$69 at the low end, $119–$900/mo for serious spend) and fit merchants spending several thousand euros or more a month. For a PT/ES SMB hub, a bid-management module is unlikely to justify a price. Profit-aware ads reporting, feed quality and marketplace retail media (Worten Ads via Mirakl Ads) are the better fits.

### Cited Findings
**Platform automation and agents**
- Performance Max: about 1 million advertisers in April 2025, per Google internal data cited by a secondary source — [mycodelesswebsite](https://mycodelesswebsite.com/performance-max-statistics/). PMax took 69% of Google Shopping spend for retail advertisers in Q4 2024 (same source). Smarter Ecommerce: PMax cost share peaked just under 82% (May 2024) and was about 76% in early 2025 — [Smarter Ecommerce](https://smarter-ecommerce.com/blog/en/google-ads/state-of-performance-max-campaigns-2025/). A 2026 audit claims PMax is 47% of all Google Ads spend (unverified methodology) — [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/performance-max-statistics-2026)
- Google's Gemini-based Ads Advisor and Analytics Advisor rolled out to all English-language Google Ads/GA accounts in early December 2025. At Google Marketing Live (May 20, 2026), **Ask Advisor** entered beta: one agent across Google Ads, Analytics, Merchant Center and GMP that can, for example, pull Merchant Center product data and set up a campaign. Broader rollout is expected by end of 2026 and a Merchant Center agent is in development — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-ask-advisor-unifies-ads-analytics-and-commerce-in-one-ai-agent/); [MediaPost](https://mediapost.com/publications/article/415219/google-rebuilds-ad-ecosystem-with-agentic-and-gemi.html); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/google-agentic-ai-ad-buying-part-of-new-industry-norm)
- Meta Advantage+: Advantage+ Shopping passed a $20B annual run-rate by Q4 2024. Advantage+ solutions reached about $60B (Q3 2025) and over $75B run-rate (Q2 2026), per secondary sources citing Meta results — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/advantage/); [MarketBeat](https://marketbeat.com/originals/advantage-shopping-metas-fast-growing-20-billion-ai-ads-star). "Nine million small businesses" use at least one Meta AI ad-creative tool (Q2 2026, secondary). Widely quoted "82% of advertisers use Advantage+" figures are untraceable to Meta (same PPC Land coverage). More than a third of ecommerce ad spend with Meta goes to Advantage+ — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/more-than-a-third-of-e-commerce-ad-spend-with-meta-goes-to-advantage/en-gb/158164)
- Amazon's agentic Seller Assistant (Sept 2025) also "generates advertising campaigns" for sellers — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/amazon/2025/amazon-expands-seller-assistant-with-agentic-ai-to-become-always-on-partner/)

Other listings give $99/$269/$649. - Feed/marketplace tools: Channable Starter $119, Standard $179, Professional $239/mo, Repricer add-on $59 — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/182568/Channable/pricing/). **Marketplace retail media (directly relevant to Worten sellers)**
- Worten runs Mirakl Ads. Sellers use it inside their existing Mirakl back office and ad costs are deducted from their marketplace balance. Mirakl's case-study headline claims "seller adoption +130%, monetization +267%" (vendor/operator claim, no method disclosed) — [Mirakl Worten case](https://www.mirakl.com/content/customer-stories/worten/)

### Inferences
- The platforms' own automation dominates. Google and Meta now give SMBs free AI agents that set up and optimize campaigns, and most Shopping/ecommerce spend already flows through black-box PMax/Advantage+. A hub's "Ads management" (bid or budget automation) would compete with free tools. Optmyzr's entry tier starting at $25K/mo spend shows that third-party optimization economics favor larger advertisers. **Recommendation:** treat Ads first as a read-only *profit lens*: ingest spend from Google, Meta and Worten Ads, then show POAS/contribution margin per channel and SKU next to order metrics. Treat feed and catalog health (Merchant Center, marketplace listings) as the actionable layer. Leave media buying to the platforms.
- For Worten-heavy merchants, sponsored-product spend on Worten (Mirakl Ads) may matter more than Google/Meta. If Mirakl exposes ad spend per seller through an API, reconciling it with order-line margin is a feature nobody else offers, but API availability is unverified.
- "Ads" may be easier to monetize as a premium tier attached to profit metrics than as a standalone module. Third-party ads tools charge $50–$330/mo even at low spend, which is likely too high for small PT merchants.

### Gaps
- No survey data found on what share of SMB merchants use third-party ads tools versus native platform UIs.
- No reliable 2025 Advantage+ adoption counts among SMBs (only revenue run-rates, from secondary sources).
- Worten Ads / Mirakl Ads seller API, minimum spend and reporting granularity: not researched.
- Google Merchant Center Next / Product Studio (free AI image and text tools) and "AI Max for Search": not covered for lack of search budget.

## (c) AI agents / copilots already shipped in ecommerce ops tools: tasks performed and pricing (included vs add-on vs credits)

### Takeaway
By late 2025 and 2026, the platforms where merchants already sell (Shopify, Amazon, Google, Mirakl) ship agentic assistants **included at no extra charge**. These assistants handle Q&A over store data, proactive suggestions, setup tasks and, with permission, actions. Separate fees show up mainly for generative content (credits: Plytix, Mirakl image optimization), for outcome-priced customer service (about $1 per resolution) and inside premium analytics suites (Triple Whale, Polar). Vendor docs themselves admit numeric errors in AI analysts.

### Inferences
- **Q&A over store data is becoming a free platform feature** (Sidekick, Amazon Seller Assistant, Google Advisor). An "AI orders analyst" that only answers questions will not justify a price on its own for a Shopify merchant. It can for a PrestaShop plus Worten merchant, who has no equivalent: PrestaShop has no native copilot found, and Mirakl's AI is operator- and catalog-facing.
- The shipped pattern for actions is **propose → human approves → execute** (Amazon "with permission", Linnworks recommends rules that the merchant builds). This matches the founder's plan of proposing Worten order-line acceptance with approval.
- Monetization pattern: analysis/Q&A is included; generation (images, bulk content) is metered by credits; customer-facing resolution is priced per outcome. That supports a "free tier with AI credits" design where credits are consumed mainly by generative or bulk jobs, not by every analyst question.

### Gaps
- BigCommerce AI features and pricing: not researched.
- Shopify Magic's specific current feature list: not separately verified.
- Klaviyo AI-specific outcome evidence: not found.
- Official Triple Whale Moby pricing or credit limits: not found (only an uncorroborated $279 add-on).
- Whether Mirakl seller APIs let third parties automate order acceptance, or whether operators like Worten allow auto-accept: not researched. This matters because, if auto-accept exists, an "AI proposes acceptance" feature adds less value than an "exception handling" feature (stock mismatch, price error, late SLA).

## (d) Evidence (not hype) of where AI decision support creates measurable value and where it fails; adoption among SMB merchants; use cases with the clearest ROI

### Takeaway
Rigorous evidence is strongest for **assistive AI in bounded tasks with a human checking**: customer support (+14% productivity in a quasi-experiment), product content and listings (very high acceptance at Amazon scale) and ops recommendations. It is weakest for **autonomous agents and free-form numeric Q&A**. Enterprise surveys show most gen-AI pilots lack P&L impact, Gartner expects over 40% of agentic projects to be cancelled by 2027, and LLMs answering business questions directly over raw schemas are often wrong (6–21% on enterprise text-to-SQL benchmarks; 16% → 54% → 72% as semantic context and checks are added). SMB AI adoption is rising but skews to larger SMBs.

### Cited Findings
**Macro and enterprise evidence**
- McKinsey State of AI 2025 (secondary summaries): about 88% of companies use AI regularly in at least one function, but only about a third have begun scaling. Only 39% report any enterprise-level EBIT impact — [Gend summary](https://gend.co/blog/mckinsey-state-of-ai-2025-key-findings-what-to-do). Another summary says 17% attribute more than 5% of EBIT to gen AI (possibly an earlier survey wave) — [VnReview](https://vnreview.vn/threads/khao-sat-mckinsey-2025-cac-to-chuc-doanh-nghiep-dang-tai-cau-truc-de-khai-thac-gia-tri-tu-ai-tao-sinh.56885/post-58523)
- McKinsey (European retail, date unconfirmed): marketing gen-AI copilots cut agency spend by about 15% and lifted conversion by up to 40%. McKinsey notes marketing's EBITDA impact is limited in retail because marketing budgets are small — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/retail/our-insights/rewiring-retail-in-europe-the-ai-imperative)
- MIT NANDA "The GenAI Divide" (July 2025): about 95% of organizations saw no measurable P&L return from gen-AI pilots, attributed to brittle workflows, no retained context or feedback, and poor fit with daily operations. Methodology is disputed: reported sample sizes vary (52 organizations / 153 leaders / 300+ public deployments) and the study is preliminary — [Computing](https://computing.co.uk/news/2025/ai/mit-report-95pc-corporate-generative-ai-pilots-fail); [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/mit-report-documents-genai-pilot-roi-gap-e0924d7d)
- Gartner (June 25, 2025): more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027 because of cost, unclear value and weak risk controls. Gartner describes widespread "agent washing", estimating only about 130 of thousands of "agentic" vendors are real. It still predicts 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously and 33% of enterprise software will include agentic AI by 2028 — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027.)

**Rigorous task-level evidence**
- Customer support: Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, "Generative AI at Work" (QJE 2025, 140(2); data from 5,179 agents, staggered rollout). Issues resolved per hour rose 14% on average, 34% for novices, with minimal effect for experienced agents. Customer sentiment and retention also improved — [NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)
- Listings and content: 900K+ Amazon sellers used gen-AI listing tools, with over 90% of AI content accepted unedited (May 2025, Amazon-reported). This is adoption and acceptance, not measured sales lift — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/08/amazons-newest-ai-tool-is-designed-to-enhance-product-listings)
- Ops automation: Linnworks reports 30+ hours/month saved for early testers who built the recommended rules (vendor, self-reported) — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260120422360/en/Linnworks-Launches-Spotlight-AI-to-Help-Online-Retailers-Automate-Operations-and-Scale-with-Confidence)
- Forecasting and pricing: a BCG case at a global fashion retailer (more than 10K products, more than 3,000 stores) reports a "double-digit profit uplift" after a 4-week pilot. This is an enterprise case, not SMB — [BCG retail](https://www.bcg.com/en-us/industries/retail/overview). A 2025 systematic review of 14 studies reports up to 23% better forecast accuracy and 17% lower inventory carrying costs (academic aggregate; quality uncertain) — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/144044593/Revolutionizing_Retail_Operations_through_Generative_AI_A_Systematic_Review). BCG/CGF say retailers use AI for pricing and assortment, demand forecasting, replenishment and marketing, with a non-representative sample — [BCG State of AI for CPG 2026](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/state-of-ai-for-cpg.pdf)

**Failure modes: numbers, trust, data**
- Enterprise text-to-SQL: on Spider 2.0, an o1-preview agent solved only 17–21.3% of tasks (version-dependent), against 91.2% on Spider 1.0 and 73% on BIRD. GPT-4 scored 6.0% on Spider 2.0-Lite. The main failures are schema linking on large schemas, SQL dialects and complex query planning — [Spider 2.0 GitHub](https://github.com/xlang-ai/Spider2); [Moonlight review](https://themoonlight.io/review/spider-20-evaluating-language-models-on-real-world-enterprise-text-to-sql-workflows)
- Semantic context helps: Sequeda et al. (2023) found GPT-4 zero-shot on raw SQL scored 16% accuracy, rising to 54% over a knowledge graph/ontology. A follow-up with ontology-based query checking and repair reached 72%, including 8% "I don't know" answers, for an implied 20% error rate. This is a single insurance-domain benchmark, and older work — [arXiv 2311.07509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07509v1); [AtScale (vendor framing)](https://www.atscale.com/blog/semantic-layers-make-genai-more-accurate)
- Production analyst agent: Triple Whale's own documentation warns about Moby's aggregation and rounding errors, entity confusion and attribution disagreements, and recommends "Show SQL" and fact-checking — [Triple Whale KB](https://kb.triplewhale.com/en/articles/11824175-moby-capabilities-limitations-and-fact-checking-guidelines). A user review says Moby is "buggy" and the data inaccurate, so they went back to native Meta and Shopify numbers (anecdotal) — [Trustpilot DK](https://dk.trustpilot.com/review/triplewhale.com). Outputs are "only as good as the inputs" and miss activity outside the vendor's ecosystem — [Putler](https://www.putler.com/?p=183196)
- Over-trust risk: the 90%-unedited acceptance of Amazon AI listings may mean sellers aren't reviewing outputs — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/08/amazons-newest-ai-tool-is-designed-to-enhance-product-listings)

**SMB adoption**
- Thryv survey (May 2025, n=540 US small businesses): AI use 55% in 2025, up from 39% in 2024; firms with 10–100 employees went from 47% to 68%; retail is among the leading sectors — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250717239434/en/AI-Adoption-Among-Small)
- Mercury holiday report (late 2025, ecommerce-oriented): 86% rely on AI "extensively or somewhat". Heavy use is 47% at 500+ employee firms against 9% at firms with 10 or fewer employees. The sample likely skews digital-savvy — [ABC17/Stacker](https://abc17news.com/stacker-money/2025/11/26/2025-ecommerce-holiday-report-headwinds-high-hopes-and-ai-acceleration/)
- Shopify Sidekick usage (100M conversations, 750K new shops in Q3 2025) is the best behavioral evidence that merchants will use conversational AI when it is free and embedded — [Insider Monkey](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/shopify-inc-nasdaqshop-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript-1640465/)
- A Statista dataset on AI adoption among small French e-tailers (March 2025, n=250) exists, but the figures sit behind a paywall — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1615419/ai-adoption-among-small-e-tailers-in-france/)

### Inferences
- **Clearest ROI for SMB merchants, ranked by strength of evidence and risk:**
  1. Product content generation, enrichment and translation (PT↔ES↔EN, marketplace attribute mapping): high adoption, cheap, easy for a human to verify. Mirakl's own investment (Catalog Transformer, Product Enrichment) shows marketplace onboarding friction is real.
  2. Customer-service assistance and deflection: the best causal evidence, with market prices around $1 per resolution.
  3. Ops rule and automation recommendations, plus anomaly and exception alerts: vendor-reported time savings and deterministic checks; low hallucination risk if alerts are rule- or statistics-based and the LLM only explains them.
  4. Q&A over metrics: high usage but the highest numeric-error risk. It needs a governed metric layer.
  5. Forecasting/reorder and pricing: credible at enterprise scale, but SMB data (few SKUs × short histories) limits accuracy, and no SMB-specific evidence was found.
- **Design implication for the "orders analyst":** never let the LLM compute numbers. Route questions to deterministic, pre-defined metrics and queries over a semantic layer (orders, order lines, channel, commission, margin), show the query or formula behind every number (as Triple Whale had to add "Show SQL"), and allow "I don't know". The 16% → 54% → 72% progression is the evidence that schema and semantic grounding plus checks decide accuracy.
- **Wedge assessment:** metrics plus AI analyst is a **strong wedge only if the data is the moat**. Unified PrestaShop plus Worten/Mirakl order-line profitability is not served by Shopify-centric tools or by Mirakl. Positioned as "chat with your orders", it competes with free platform copilots and with the 95%/40% failure narrative. The proposed approval-gated actions (accept Worten order lines, flag at-risk SLAs) fit the "with permission" pattern Amazon ships. Measure value as minutes saved per day and SLA or cancellation-rate improvement, not as "insights".

### Gaps
- No independent, systematic accuracy audit of any ecommerce AI analyst (Moby, Sidekick, Ask Polar) was found.
- No SMB-specific causal evidence on demand forecasting, anomaly alerts or pricing AI.
- Portugal/Spain-specific SMB AI adoption (e.g., Eurostat 2025 enterprise AI use by country and size class): the search budget ran out before this could be gathered.
- The eBay machine-translation trade study (Brynjolfsson, Hui & Liu) would support the translation-ROI claim, but the search was blocked, so it cannot be cited here.
- No Shopify, Gartner or BCG data specific to SMB merchants' trust in AI-generated numbers was found.

## (e) Unit economics: typical AI cost per task and how vendors price AI (seats, credits, included quotas)

### Takeaway
Raw model cost for an analyst question is a fraction of a cent on small models and roughly $0.05–$0.30 on frontier models (estimate). Vendors price far above cost: about $1 per support resolution, per-action credits for content, or AI bundled into GMV-tiered suites. A free tier with an AI-credit allowance is economically feasible if routine Q&A runs on a small model with cached context and heavy generation (images, bulk catalog rewrites) uses credits.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic first-party API prices per million tokens (input/output): Claude Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50 (prompts ≤100K; $0.50/$2.50 beyond); Claude Haiku 4.5 $1/$5; Claude Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10; Claude Opus 5.5 $4/$20. Cache reads cost about 0.1x input ($0.20/M on Sonnet/Opus 5.5) and the Batch API is 50% off — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing.md); [Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching.md) (taken from Anthropic's bundled API reference, cached 2026-10-06)

Caching discounts reach up to 90% and batch about 50%; reasoning tokens raise effective output cost — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/llm-api-pricing-cost-per-task-comparison); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/llm-api-pricing-comparison-2026-8/)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative cost per "orders analyst" question** (my estimate from the prices above; not measured). Assumptions: about 3–5 tool calls, about 30–50K input tokens in total across the loop (system prompt, tool schemas and metric catalog mostly cached, plus query results) and about 2–4K output tokens including reasoning.
  - Haiku 5.5: about 40K × $0.10/M + 3K × $0.50/M ≈ **$0.005**
  - Sonnet 5.5: about $0.08 + $0.03 ≈ **$0.05–$0.12**, lower with cache hits
  - Opus 5.5: ≈ **$0.15–$0.30**
  - At 100 questions per tenant per month, that is about $0.50 (Haiku) to $5–$12 (Sonnet). A free tier with something like 50–100 analyst questions a month is affordable on a small model. Proactive daily digests can run through the Batch API at 50% off.
- Biggest cost risks: long raw result sets pasted into context (use aggregation tools and return compact tables), uncached system prompts, agent loops that retry, and reasoning-heavy models. Price by **credits per action class**: Q&A cheap, bulk content and image work expensive, approved actions (e.g., accepting order lines) possibly free to encourage use. This mirrors the Plytix and outcome-pricing patterns.
- Vendor price points (about $1 per resolution; $399/mo AI add-ons) against sub-cent model costs show AI features carry very high gross margins. Merchants pay for **outcomes and trust**, not tokens. That favors "AI included with a fair-use cap" for analysis, with metered credits only for generation.

### Gaps
- No vendor discloses its actual cost per AI task; all cost-per-task numbers above are my estimates.
- Official pricing pages for Gemini and OpenAI were not checked (secondary tables conflict).
- No data was gathered on PT/ES merchants' willingness to pay for AI features or their monthly SaaS budgets (likely covered by another researcher).
